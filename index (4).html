This calendar shows every planned post for LinkedIn, Instagram and X from January to June 2027. Here is how to use it week to week.

Using the calendar

  1. Pick a month with the buttons above the calendar. Each month has a focus written on the right.
  2. Show or hide a platform by clicking LinkedIn, Instagram or X at the top. Hide two to see one platform's plan on its own.
  3. Click any post to see what to post, how to write the caption, and what to do after it goes up.
  4. Open the Canva layout from inside the post. Each post tells you which file and which page to use. Make a copy before editing so the template stays clean.
  5. Save your own Canva link in any post if your team makes a better version. It applies to every post of that type, and on the Claude link everyone sees it.
  6. Move a post by dragging it to another day. To move it to a different month, open the post and pick a new date at the bottom. On a phone, use the date picker.
  7. Moves are shared on the Claude link. Anyone with edit access can move posts, and everyone sees the change right away with the person's name, like "Moved by Kaylee." People with view-only access see the moves but can't make them. On the GitHub copy, moves save only in your own browser.
  8. Undo moves with the "Undo all moves" button (click it twice to confirm). This puts every post back for everyone, so check with the team first.
  9. Download Excel to get every post from January to June in a spreadsheet, with a tab for each platform and empty Status and Notes columns for tracking. Moved posts show their new date and who moved them.
  10. Fill in the brackets. Anything in [brackets] needs a teacher name, photo, event or deadline from LDC before it posts.

What each platform does

LinkedIn

2x a week
  • For district and state leaders who choose curriculum and PD
  • Tuesday is always an event recap with photos of people
  • Thursday rotates tips, teacher stories, partner wins and stats
  • End posts with a question
  • Ask staff to repost every week (1.5x more engagement)

Instagram

2x a week
  • For teachers, school staff, parents and donors
  • Monday: one carousel (mini lesson or educator spotlight)
  • Thursday: one Reel of a classroom moment
  • Add a few Stories each week, especially at events
  • Carousels get 43% more engagement than single images

X

1x a week
  • For education reporters, researchers and policymakers
  • Wednesday rotates a stat chart, an event recap, and replies
  • Put links in the first reply, not the post
  • Pin one post that explains what LDC does
  • Charts get 2.5x more engagement

Writing posts so people find them

  • Write out "Literacy Design Collaborative" at least once in every post. Google and AI tools have a hard time with "LDC."
  • Spell out program names like Dialogue 250 and EIR the first time.
  • Instagram captions: use the words teachers search, like "civic discussion lesson middle school," and the same few hashtags every time.
  • X links go in the first reply. Posts with links can get 30 to 50% less reach.
  • Get photo releases first before posting students or teachers.
  • Educator spotlights go up as Instagram Collab posts so they show on the teacher's account too.

Team routine

  • Monthly: plan next month's posts a month ahead and fill in every [bracket].
  • Weekly: check how last week's posts did and remind staff to repost.
  • Monthly: update the results dashboard, and ask ChatGPT and Claude "What is Literacy Design Collaborative?" If the answer is wrong, fix the page it came from.
  • Every quarter: review what's working and adjust the plan.

Before any post goes out

  1. Is it clear who this is for?
  2. Is there one main point?
  3. Is there a photo of real people or a simple chart?
  4. Does every number have a source?
  5. Is "Literacy Design Collaborative" written out?
  6. Does it end with a question or one clear next step?
  7. For recruitment: does it say what people get, what it takes, and the deadline?
